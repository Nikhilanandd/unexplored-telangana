# Ramappa Temple (Rudreshwara)

_A 13th-century masterpiece and Telangana's only UNESCO World Heritage Site_

## The Architecture

The Ramappa Temple is the crowning achievement of Kakatiya architecture. The temple stands on a 6-foot-high star-shaped platform, built from red sandstone and black basalt — a combination that gives it a distinctive two-tone appearance. The main shrine is dedicated to Rudreshwara (Shiva), with a majestic Nandi mandapam facing it.

### The Floating Bricks

The temple's most remarkable engineering feat is its roof — constructed from lightweight "floating bricks" made from a mixture of clay, wood, and other materials. These bricks float on water and significantly reduce the roof's weight, making the structure earthquake-resistant. Visitors can see and touch these bricks at the demonstration area near the entrance.

### The Bracket Figures

The temple is adorned with 12 bracket figures — life-size sculptures of dancing women in various poses. Each figure is carved from a single block of basalt and polished to a mirror-like finish. The _Mandakini_ (celestial nymphs) are considered among the finest examples of Kakatiya sculpture, rivaling the bracket figures of Belur and Halebidu.

### The Nandi Mandapam

The monolithic black basalt Nandi (Shiva's bull) sits in a separate mandapam directly facing the main shrine. Unlike typical Nandi sculptures, this one is elaborately decorated with bells, necklaces, and anklets carved from the same stone block.

## UNESCO Recognition

In July 2021, UNESCO inscribed the Kakatiya Rudreshwara (Ramappa) Temple as a World Heritage Site under Criteria I and III:

- **Criterion I:** The temple represents a masterpiece of human creative genius
- **Criteria III:** It bears exceptional testimony to the Kakatiya cultural tradition

The temple was India's 39th World Heritage Site and Telangana's first — a monumental recognition of the state's architectural heritage.

## Visitor Information

- **Timings:** 6:00 AM – 6:00 PM daily
- **Entry Fee:** Free (ASI monument)
- **Photography:** Allowed (no flash inside the sanctum)
- **Best Months:** November – February
- **Average Visit Duration:** 2–3 hours

## How to Reach

- **From Hyderabad:** 210 km via NH 163 (~4 hours). Take the Warangal bypass and continue towards Mulugu
- **From Warangal:** 65 km via Mulugu Road (~1.5 hours)
- **From Hanamkonda:** 70 km via Hunter Road
- **Nearest Railway Station:** Warangal (65 km)
