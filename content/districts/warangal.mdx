## History

Warangal, originally known as Orugallu (meaning "single stone"), served as the capital of the Kakatiya dynasty from the 12th to 14th centuries. Under rulers like Ganapati Deva and Rudrama Devi — one of India's rare female monarchs — the Kakatiyas built an empire that stretched across the Deccan. The city flourished as a center of Telugu culture, literature, and temple architecture.

The iconic Warangal Fort, with its four massive ornamental gateways (Kakatiya Kala Thoranam), stands as a testament to the kingdom's architectural genius. Marco Polo visited the Kakatiya kingdom and documented its prosperity. After the dynasty's fall to the Delhi Sultanate in 1323, Warangal passed through the Bahmani, Qutb Shahi, and Asaf Jahi periods.

## Must-Visit Locations

### 1. Ramappa Temple (UNESCO World Heritage)
A 13th-century architectural marvel built during the reign of Kakatiya king Ganapati Deva. The temple features floating bricks that were used in its roof construction, making it lightweight and earthquake-resistant. The intricate carvings, bracket figures of dancing women, and the Nandi mandapam are extraordinary.

### 2. Thousand Pillar Temple
Built in 1163 CE by Rudra Deva, this star-shaped temple dedicated to Shiva, Vishnu, and Surya is a masterpiece of Kakatiya architecture. Despite its name, the temple has about 300 intricately carved pillars, each unique in design. The temple is built on a raised platform and features outstanding sculptural work.

### 3. Warangal Fort
The sprawling fort complex spread over 19 kilometers, with massive stone gateways of the Kakatiya Kala Thoranam — now the official emblem of Telangana. The fort contains ruins of temples, ceremonial halls, and the legendary Svayambhu Shiva temple.

### 4. Bhadrakali Temple
Located on a hilltop overlooking the Bhadrakali Lake, this temple is one of the oldest in Telangana, dedicated to the goddess Bhadrakali. The temple houses a 2.7-meter-tall stone idol in a seated position with fierce eyes.

### 5. Pakhal Lake
A serene man-made lake built in 1213 CE by Kakatiya king Ganapati Deva, surrounded by the Pakhal Wildlife Sanctuary. Perfect for birdwatching, boating, and sunset photography.

### 6. Kakatiya Musical Garden
A beautifully landscaped garden with a synchronized musical fountain show in the evenings, located near the Bhadrakali Temple. A popular evening spot for families.

### 7. Kakatiya Rock Garden
A geological park featuring naturally formed rock formations, waterfalls (seasonal), and caves. Great for nature hikes and photography.

### 8. Eturnagaram Wildlife Sanctuary
One of the oldest wildlife sanctuaries in Telangana, known for its tigers, leopards, sloth bears, and diverse bird species. Best visited during winter months.

### 9. Laknavaram Lake
A picturesque lake with suspension bridge access to an island resort. Operated by Telangana Tourism, it offers boating, cottages, and camping facilities.

### 10. Medaram (Sammakka Saralamma Jatara)
Hosts Asia's largest tribal congregation — the Sammakka Saralamma Jatara — held every two years. Millions of devotees gather to worship the tribal goddesses Sammakka and Saralamma.

## Travel Tips
- Start early (6 AM) to cover Ramappa, Thousand Pillar, and Fort in one day
- Hire a government-approved guide at Ramappa Temple for the full story
- Carry water and wear comfortable walking shoes — the fort area is vast
- Try Warangal's famous "Pootharekulu" (rice paper sweets) from local sweet shops
- Visit Laknavaram or Pakhal Lake for sunset; both are stunning
- Medaram Jatara happens every two years in February — plan accordingly if interested

## Getting There
- **By Road:** 140 km from Hyderabad via NH 163 (2.5 hours)
- **By Train:** Warangal Railway Station is a major junction on the Chennai–Delhi route
- **By Air:** Nearest airport is Hyderabad (RGIA), 150 km away
- **Local Transport:** Auto-rickshaws, cabs, and TSRTC buses available
